Installation — Get Up and Running

✅ How to install Consentaro (the easy way)

Installing Consentaro is just like installing any other free WordPress plugin. Here's how:

1
Go to your WordPress Dashboard

Log in to your WordPress admin panel (usually at yoursite.com/wp-admin).

2
Click Plugins → Add New

In the left sidebar, click Plugins, then click Add New Plugin.

3
Search for "Consentaro"

Type Consentaro in the search box. The plugin should appear at the top of the results.

4
Click Install Now, then Activate

Click the blue Install Now button, wait a few seconds, then click Activate.

5
You're done! Follow the Guide

After activation, you'll be automatically taken to Consentaro → Guide — a friendly walkthrough that tells you exactly what to set up next.

💡

Test in a private/incognito window! After setting everything up, open your website in an incognito window (Ctrl+Shift+N / Cmd+Shift+N). You should see the cookie banner appear. Click Accept or Deny — the banner should disappear and not come back in the same window.

General Settings — The On/Off Controls

What you'll find under Consentaro → General

✓

Enable ConsentaroThe main on/off switch. Keep this ON for the plugin to work. If you turn it off, no banner shows and consent signals stop firing.

✓

GTM Container IDOptional. If you use Google Tag Manager, paste your container ID here (looks like GTM-XXXXXXX). Consentaro will load it only after the visitor makes a choice.

✓

Geo-Based BannerWhen enabled, the banner only shows to visitors from EU/EEA/UK/CH countries. Visitors from other countries won't see it. Great for international sites!

✓

Script BlockingAutomatically holds third-party tracking scripts (Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, etc.) until the visitor gives consent. Recommended to keep ON.

✓

Consent LogOff by default. Turn on if you want a record of consent decisions (useful for compliance audits). Records are anonymized — no IP addresses stored.

✓

Cloudflare Site ToggleOnly enable if your site is actually behind Cloudflare. This restores faster geo-detection using Cloudflare headers.

🎯

For most sites, just turn on Enable Consentaro and leave everything else at defaults. You can always come back and fine-tune later!

Go to Consentaro → Design. Everything here has a live preview so you can see changes instantly.

⚠️

Script Blocking — What It Does & Why It Matters

What is script blocking?

When script blocking is ON, Consentaro automatically detects and "pauses" tracking scripts on your site until the visitor gives consent. Think of it like a bouncer at a club — the tracking scripts are held at the door until the visitor says yes.

It works for both external scripts (loaded from remote URLs) and inline scripts (code pasted directly into your theme or page builder).

Which scripts are automatically detected?

G

Google Analytics (GA4)analytics_storage category

M

Meta / Facebook Pixelad_storage + ad_user_data category

T

TikTok Pixelad_storage category

L

LinkedIn Insight Tagad_storage category

H

Hotjaranalytics_storage category

🛡️

Your site's own scripts are NEVER touched. Consentaro only blocks scripts it recognizes as third-party trackers. Your theme, page builder, WooCommerce, payment gateways, and everything else always loads normally.

How to manage scripts manually

Go to Consentaro → Scripts. You'll see a list of every third-party script detected on your site. You can:

1
See what was auto-detected

Each script shows its URL or identifier and the consent category it was automatically assigned to.

2
Override the category

If a script was assigned to the wrong category, use the dropdown to change it. For example, if a chat widget was tagged as "advertising" but it's really "functional."

Google Tag Manager — The Simple Setup

Do I even need GTM?

No, GTM is completely optional! Consentaro works perfectly without it. The cookie banner and Google Consent Mode signals work on their own.

You'd want to add your GTM ID if you manage Google Analytics, Ads pixels, or other tags through Google Tag Manager.

How to connect Google Tag Manager

1
Find your GTM Container ID

Log in to tagmanager.google.com. Your Container ID looks like GTM-XXXXXXX and appears at the top of the screen.

2
Paste it in Consentaro

Go to Consentaro → General and paste your Container ID into the GTM Container ID field. Must match the pattern GTM-XXXXXXX exactly.

3
Save and test

Click Save Settings. In an incognito window, click Accept on the banner. GTM should now load and fire your tags. Verify in GTM Preview mode.

✨

Consentaro sends Google Consent Mode default signals before GTM loads, so GA4 and Google Ads modelling still works even for visitors who deny consent!

Geo-Based Display — Show Banner Only Where Needed

What does "Geo-Based Banner" do?

When this is enabled, the cookie banner only appears for visitors from countries where cookie consent laws apply — specifically EU/EEA countries, UK, and Switzerland. Visitors from the US, Canada, or other regions won't see any banner.

This is great for global sites that want to stay compliant without annoying visitors who don't need to see a consent dialog.

I turned Geo on but the banner doesn't appear anymore. Why?

You're probably testing from outside the EU. To test the banner, you can temporarily turn Geo off in General settings — the banner will show for all visitors. Remember to turn it back on after testing!

⚠️

If your site is behind Cloudflare, enable the "This site is behind Cloudflare" toggle in General settings. This restores fast, accurate geo detection.

Consent Log — Proof of Consent Records

What is the Consent Log?

The Consent Log is an optional feature (off by default) that keeps a record of every consent decision made by visitors. Think of it as a receipt book — every time a visitor clicks Accept, Deny, or Customize, it writes down what happened.

Is it privacy-safe?

Yes, by design. The Consent Log stores zero personal information:

✓

TimestampWhen the decision was made

✓

Categories Granted/DeniedWhich consent categories were accepted or rejected

✓

Random Token (RID)A random number to track if the same browser changed its choice later. NOT linked to any person.

✗

NO IP AddressNever stored. Never. Period.

How to enable and use it

1
Enable in General Settings

Go to Consentaro → General and toggle on "Keep a log of consent decisions."

2
Set the retention period

Choose how long to keep records (1–24 months). Default is 12 months. Old records are automatically deleted.

3
Export as CSV (Free)

Export all consent records as a CSV file for audits or legal compliance reviews.

4
View & Filter in Pro

Pro Upgrade to Consentaro Pro to browse, search, and filter consent records directly in your wp-admin dashboard.

Insights — Understand Your Consent Rates

What does the Insights tab show?

The Consentaro → Insights tab shows you how many visitors each day chose Accept All, Deny All, or Customize their consent. You can view data for the last 7, 30, or 90 days in a simple chart.

🔒

No visitor tracking involved. Insights only stores anonymous daily totals (how many Accept / Deny / Customize clicks happened). It has no idea who clicked — just that someone did.

What can I learn from it?

If your Deny rate is very high, it might be worth revisiting your banner text to make it friendlier and more transparent. If almost no one uses Customize, you might simplify the options. These are useful signals for improving your consent flow!

WooCommerce — Smart Event Gating

How does Consentaro work with WooCommerce?

If your site runs WooCommerce and you use Google Tag Manager or Google Analytics for ecommerce tracking, Consentaro automatically gates the following dataLayer events:

🛒

view_itemWhen a visitor views a product page

🛒

add_to_cartWhen a visitor adds a product to their cart

🛒

purchaseWhen an order is completed

These events are only pushed to the dataLayer when the visitor's analytics consent is granted. This prevents tracking data from firing without permission.

🎯

No configuration needed. If WooCommerce is active, this feature works automatically. There's nothing to turn on — it just works.

Translations & Multilingual Sites

Using WPML or Polylang?

Consentaro works seamlessly with both WPML and Polylang. The banner text can be set per language from each plugin's String Translation screen.

1
Set your default banner text

Write your banner text in your main language under Consentaro → Design.

2
Translate via WPML / Polylang

In WPML: go to Languages → String Translations, search for "Consentaro," and translate the banner text for each language. Same process for Polylang.

🌐

All other button labels (Accept All, Deny All, Customize, etc.) are translated automatically through WordPress's standard translation system — no extra setup needed!

Quick FAQ — Common Questions

I don't see the banner. What's wrong?

Try these steps:

1
Test in a private/incognito window

Your browser may have already saved a consent choice. Incognito starts fresh.

2
Check if the plugin is enabled

Go to Consentaro → General and confirm "Enable Consentaro" is toggled ON.

3
Turn off Geo-Based Banner temporarily

If Geo is ON and you're testing from outside the EU, the banner won't show. Turn it off to test.

Will this replace my privacy policy?

No. Consentaro handles the technical side of cookie consent and Google Consent Mode signals. It does not generate a privacy policy. You still need a proper privacy policy for your site — either write one yourself or use a service for that.

Does it track individual visitors?

No. Consentaro never stores IP addresses, user agents, or any personal identifiers. The Insights tab only stores anonymous daily totals. The Consent Log (if you enable it) uses a random, non-identifying token — nothing that could identify a real person.

Will script blocking break my website?

No. Consentaro is very conservative — it only blocks scripts it specifically recognizes as third-party trackers. Your theme, page builder, WooCommerce, payment gateway scripts, and anything Consentaro doesn't recognize is always left completely alone.
